Website conversion review

What malihaus.com is losing, and where to get it back

Prepared for George Munoz, MaliHaus Capital Prepared by Andrew Cherry, RequityAI Date 24 August 2026

George, following our conversation on 21 August, I went through malihaus.com the way a seller would, then went through it again the way a search engine does. The offer itself is good. Cash, as is, no fees, no commission, and a genuine point of difference in that you can route a house through several outlets instead of one. None of that is the problem.

The problem is that almost none of it reaches the person on the page, and what does reach them has no way to turn into a conversation unless they fill in a seven field form and then wait. Seventeen findings follow, grouped by where in the seller's journey they bite. Each one has what I would do about it.

Part one

What happens when a seller lands

Distressed and motivated sellers do not shop for weeks. They contact two or three buyers in an evening and go with whoever responds first and feels safest. Everything in this section is about that window.

01Critical

You have 29 five star reviews and not one of them appears on your website

Your Google Business Profile carries a 5.0 average across 29 reviews. That is a genuinely strong record and it is the single most persuasive asset you own. On the website it is invisible. The page headed Testimonials contains no testimonials at all. It carries four press quotes from Sold.com, Forbes, Realty Biz News and Home World Design, and a link that sends the visitor off your site to read reviews somewhere else.

Every visitor you send that page is being asked to leave in order to find out whether you are trustworthy. Most will not come back.

What to do

Pull the live Google reviews onto the site and keep them syncing, so new ones appear on their own. Put a rating band in the header where it is visible before any scrolling, three named reviews next to the form, and the full set on the Testimonials page. Add the review markup so the stars can also show in Google results.

02Critical

Nothing on the site responds to a seller who does not fill in the form

There is no chat, no instant reply, no callback option, and no path at all outside business hours. The site has exactly two doors, a phone number and a long form, and both of them require the seller to make the effort and then wait for a human.

A seller reading your page at 9pm about a house they inherited has no way to ask one question. They close the tab.

What to do

Add a conversation layer that answers immediately, day or night, and can qualify the property, answer the common questions and hand a warm lead to you. Extend it to the phone as well, so that a call you miss is answered by text within seconds rather than sitting as a voicemail. This runs on the site, on text, and on your Google listing.

03High

There is no way for a seller to book a time

The stated process is call us or fill in the form and our team will contact you. That leaves the seller with no control and no certainty, and it puts the burden of chasing on your side.

What to do

Let a seller pick a slot on your calendar directly from the page, with a confirmation and reminders by text so they turn up. Booked time on a calendar converts at a different rate to a form entry, because the seller has already committed.

04Critical

The form asks for a great deal before it gives anything

The home page carries the same form four times. Each one asks for full address, street line, city, state, ZIP, full name, email and phone, with almost all of it required.

The state field is a dropdown of all fifty states plus Guam, Puerto Rico, the US Virgin Islands, American Samoa, the Northern Mariana Islands and three Armed Forces regions. For a Florida buyer, on a phone, that is a sixty item scroll to answer a question you already know the answer to.

Every extra required field costs completions. Asking for all of them before the seller has any sense of what they will get costs more.

What to do

Ask for the address first and nothing else, then reveal the rest in short steps once the seller is committed. Default the state to Florida. Cut the required fields on the first step to one.

05High

The seller gets nothing back for their address

They hand over the property and their contact details and receive a confirmation that someone will be in touch. There is no range, no estimate, no comparison, no indication of which of your several exit routes might suit them.

What to do

Return something immediately. Even a preliminary range with a clear explanation that a firm number follows a short conversation is enough to justify the form. It also makes the follow up call a continuation rather than a cold start.

06Moderate

On a phone there is no persistent way to call you

The number sits in the header. Once the seller scrolls, it is gone, and the page is roughly five and a half thousand pixels tall.

What to do

Fix a call bar and a text option to the bottom of the screen on mobile so both are always one tap away, and track which one gets used.

Part two

Whether the seller believes you

A seller handing over a house to a company they found online is making a large and frightening decision. The page has to answer who you are, what you have done, and why you rather than the other three.

07Critical

There is no evidence on the site that you have ever bought a house

The home page carries eleven images. Six of them are generic line icons served from a stock library. There are no property photographs, no before and after, no closing statements, no number of homes bought, no dollar figures, no map of what you have done, and no photograph of you or anyone on your team.

The About page says the company is family owned, has been in business more than ten years and has helped hundreds of homeowners. Those are exactly the claims that need proof next to them, and none is offered.

What to do

Put real work on the page. Photographs of houses you have bought, the situation each seller was in, how long it took to close. A named owner with a photograph and a short account of why you do this. That combination, next to the Google reviews, is what separates you from the next tab.

08High

The strongest thing you have is buried in a paragraph

Most cash buyers make one offer and that is the whole conversation. You can route a house through your own capital, through local renovators, or through national auction and buyer platforms, which means you can create competition for a property rather than simply pricing it. That is a real advantage and most of your competitors cannot claim it.

On the page it appears once, in the middle, in a short paragraph headed Our Advantage, underneath a row of stock icons that say the same generic things every other buyer says.

What to do

Make it the headline idea, not a footnote. Show the routes side by side with what each one means for the seller in money and in time. It is the only claim on the site that a seller cannot get from the buyer down the road.

09Moderate

The site is recognizably the same template other buyers use

The layout, the icon set and the section structure come from a shared template that a large number of other investor websites also run. A seller comparing three buyers in one evening will see three versions of the same page, which makes price the only visible difference between you.

What to do

Rebuild the home page around your own brand, your own photographs, your reviews and your multiple exit routes. This is the piece that also makes every other fix in this document possible, because the current template controls what can and cannot be put on the page.

Part three

What Google and AI search can see

More sellers now start with a question typed into an AI assistant than with a search results page. Both routes read the same signals, and several of yours currently contradict each other.

10Critical

Three different phone numbers are in public for one business

(321) 461-2550 is on the website header and every call button
(321) 655-2099 is in the consent text under every form on the site
(407) 917-3347 is on your Google Business Profile

Consistency of name, address and phone number across your listings is one of the things local ranking is built on, and yours does not match. It also means you cannot tell which number produced which lead, and a seller who sees a different number on Google to the one on the website has a small reason to hesitate.

What to do

Settle on one primary number, correct it everywhere it appears, and put tracking behind it so calls from the website, from Google and from ads are separated in the reporting.

11High

The location story does not hold together

The page title and search description say Boca Raton
The headline says Florida Sellers
The body copy says Central Florida renovators and Central Florida buyers
The About page says you buy in Broward County
Your Google listing is a Boca Raton address with an Orlando area code

A seller in Boca Raton reads Central Florida and wonders whether you are local. A seller in Orlando reads Boca Raton and wonders the same. Search engines resolve the ambiguity by trusting none of it strongly.

What to do

Pick the primary market and lead with it everywhere, then let the other counties live on their own genuinely distinct pages rather than in the headline.

12High

The ten county links all point at the same Boca Raton page

The footer lists Miami Dade, Broward, Pinellas, Hillsborough, Orange, Seminole, Polk, Pasco, Duval and Palm Beach. Every one of those links resolves to a numbered variant of the Boca Raton page.

So a page labeled Duval County, which is Jacksonville, roughly three hundred miles from Boca Raton, is a copy of a Boca Raton page. Duplicated pages of this kind do not rank, and they can hold back the pages that would.

What to do

Either build genuinely distinct pages for the markets you actually work, with local content and local proof on each, or reduce the list to the markets you serve properly. Ten thin copies are worth less than two real ones.

13High

One Google profile is being asked to serve two opposite sellers

Your Business Profile category is Real estate agency. If you also take listings, that category is correct for the listing side of the business and should stay. The difficulty is that the same profile is the front door for the cash purchase side, where the seller has usually come looking specifically to avoid an agent. They search for a buyer and are shown an agency.

The same split runs through the rest of it. One website sells a cash purchase, a second site sells investor deals under the seller site's title, and one Google listing sits behind both.

What to do

This is a decision to make before anything is changed, not a setting to flip. Once we know which side this profile is meant to represent, and whether the listing side is going to advertise in its own right, the category, the description and the services follow from that. Whichever way it goes, three things are worth doing now: add the secondary categories you are currently missing, complete the profile properly with services, hours, description, photographs and answered questions, and start the review flow from it.

14Moderate

There is nothing on the site for an AI assistant to quote

When someone asks an assistant how to sell an inherited house in Florida without repairs, it answers from pages that address that specific situation in plain language and mark it up so it can be read as a question and an answer. Your site has no such markup, and no pages built around the situations sellers actually arrive in: probate, inherited, behind on payments, divorce, relocation, a tired rental, a house that will not pass inspection.

What to do

Build a small set of situation pages, each written for one circumstance and each ending in the same booking step, plus proper question and answer markup. This is the work that makes you visible in AI answers, and it compounds rather than stopping when a budget stops.

15Moderate

A second site of yours carries the identical page title

buyershaus.com, which is your investor facing site, uses exactly the same page title as malihaus.com: We Buy Houses, Sell Your Home in Boca Raton, MaliHaus Capital. It is a different audience and a different offer wearing the seller site's title.

What to do

Give the investor site its own title and description written for buyers, so the two stop competing for the same search and each one is legible for what it is.

Part four

What you can measure

This part is short and it is the one that limits everything else, because none of the work above can be judged without it.

16Critical

Nothing on the website is measured

I checked the whole page, both the code it serves and the requests it makes. There is no Google Analytics, no Google Tag Manager, no Meta pixel, no advertising tag of any kind, and no call tracking. The only thing recording anything is your website provider's own page counter.

Practically, this means: you cannot tell how many people visit, where they came from, how many reach a form, how many abandon it, which page produced a lead, or what any lead cost. If you spend money on traffic today, none of it can be attributed.
What to do

Put proper measurement in before anything else changes, so there is a baseline to compare against. Analytics and tag management, conversion events on every form, call and text, call tracking behind the phone number, and one weekly report that shows visits, leads, calls, cost and source in one place.

17Moderate

The page loads a large amount of software it never uses

The site delivers its editing and media management code to ordinary visitors, roughly a hundred scripts in total, including the whole page builder and the administrator toolbar styling. None of it does anything for a seller and all of it has to download first.

Speed matters most on the exact traffic you want, which is someone on a phone, on mobile data, in a hurry.

What to do

Handled by the rebuild in finding 09. A purpose built page loads a fraction of this.

Where to start

The first four moves, in order

Ordered by what returns the most for the least, not by what is most interesting to build.

  1. Measurement first

    Analytics, conversion tracking and call tracking, so everything after this can be judged against a real baseline rather than an opinion. This takes days, not weeks.

  2. Put the reviews on the site and fix the response layer

    Your 5.0 rating goes where visitors see it, and a conversation layer starts answering instantly on the site, by text and on missed calls. These two together address the largest share of what is currently being lost.

  3. Rebuild the home page

    Your brand, your photographs, your reviews, your multiple exit routes as the leading idea, a short staged form that starts with the address alone, and booking straight into your calendar. This is also what unlocks the findings the current template blocks.

  4. Correct the record on Google, then build the situation pages

    One phone number everywhere, a complete profile, the missing secondary categories and a steady flow of new reviews, once we have settled which side of the business the profile represents. Then the situation pages, which are what put you in AI answers and keep working after that.

None of this is a criticism of the business. The offer is sound, the reviews say the service is genuinely good, and the multiple exit routes are a real advantage. The gap is entirely between what is true about MaliHaus and what a seller on the website can actually see and do.
